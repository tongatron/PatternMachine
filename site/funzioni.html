Getting started

  1. Choose the machine from the Machine menu below the Save bar (SP-1200, RX-5, TR-808, TR-909, TR-707, TR-727, TR-606, CR-78, CR-8000, LinnDrum, DMX, Drumulator, DrumTraks, SDS-V): it changes the sound of every row. The default is the TR-808; the machine you choose is remembered next time. About the machines leads to their history.
  2. Load a beat: in the Pattern panel press Browse…, filter by style and press Load. Or start from an empty grid with + New.
  3. Play (or the space bar) starts playback; press it again for Pause, then Resume from the same position. Stop returns to the beginning; Loop repeats the current Pattern or Song. Count-in adds one or two metronome bars before playback. While it plays you can turn steps on and off: you hear the change on the next pass.
  4. Edit: clicking a step turns it on or off. Above the grid, "Step click" sets what a click does (Probability, Ratchets, Flam, Tuning, Decay, Filter).
  5. All the options of a row: click the track name. Below the steps a lane opens for each parameter (Level, Probability, Ratchets, Flam, Tuning, Decay, Filter); click a cell = next value, alt+click = back to default. One row opens at a time.
  6. Song: in the Sequencer tab, chain the patterns, each with its own repeats, and press Song next to Play.
  7. Save the project; from the Export menu download MIDI, WAV or a package for Logic, Ableton Live, REAPER or other DAWs.

How to read a beat

Every row is one sound and every cell is one moment. In a 16-step 4/4 bar, steps 1–4 are beat 1, 5–8 beat 2, 9–12 beat 3 and 13–16 beat 4.

ElementWhere to lookRole
Kick1, 5, 9, 13Weight and the groove's reference point.
Snare5, 13Answer to the kick and backbeat.
Closed hi-hatstep pari o tutti i 16 stepSubdivision and movement.

A recommended workflow

  1. Start with kick and snare: listen to the pattern before filling it.
  2. Add the hi-hat and balance its level before adding more percussion.
  3. Use accents and ghost notes for dynamics; use Probability and Ratchets only where they help.
  4. Save a stable version before trying Vary, Lock or Arrange.

Connecting to DAWs, MIDI and clock

The Export menu is the export hub for Logic, Ableton Live, REAPER and other DAWs. The macOS version of PATTERN-MACHINE also creates the virtual MIDI port PatternMachine. The port works with Logic Pro, Ableton Live, REAPER, FL Studio, Cubase, Bitwig, Studio One and any other DAW that handles MIDI.

Playing your DAW's instruments with PATTERN-MACHINE

  1. In your DAW, open a track with a drum instrument and enable monitoring or record.
  2. In the macOS app turn on MIDI output. For drum racks you can keep Channel 10; with Separate channels each row goes out on its own MIDI channel from the PatternMachine virtual port.
  3. Choose General MIDI for drum racks and compatible instruments (36 kick, 38 snare, 42 closed hi-hat, 46 open hi-hat). SP-1200 kit (36+) is for the SP-1200 kit built specifically in Logic.
  4. Play the pattern or the song. The app's pads and keys can be recorded in the DAW as MIDI notes.

Making the app follow the DAW's tempo and transport

  1. In the DAW, enable sending MIDI Clock to the PatternMachine MIDI port. If available, also enable Start/Stop/Continue and Song Position Pointer.
  2. In the app's DAW panel, choose the DAW that is sending clock and turn on Follow DAW clock. The choice only makes the connection status easier to read: the protocol received is standard MIDI Clock for every DAW.
  3. Press Play in the DAW. The app follows Play, Stop, Continue, cycle position and tempo; every sixteenth note is derived from the MIDI clock, so the two programs never drift apart.

If clock is arriving but the app doesn't start, check that the DAW is also sending transport messages and that the destination really is PatternMachine. It's best to send clock from one DAW at a time.

Export and offline workflow

  • MIDI pattern and MIDI song use the General MIDI map, the most compatible choice across DAWs. The MIDI file includes tempo, position, swing, feel and ratchets.
  • WAV files and stems are aligned from the start: drag them in together at bar 1 of the DAW.
  • MIDI Clock is for playing the app in real time with the DAW; you don't need it to import an exported MIDI file.

Recording without losing the groove

  1. Start Play and turn on Rec before playing with keys or pads.
  2. Play slightly before the desired step: recording chooses the nearest step.
  3. Turn Rec off when you are done, then correct accents directly in the grid.

MIDI keyboard in the browser

A USB MIDI keyboard (ready-made assignments for the AKAI MPK mini Play mk3 and the Arturia MiniLab 3, any other works too) works right on the site in Chrome, Edge and Firefox; Safari can't read MIDI devices. Open the MIDI tab and press Connect:

  • the keys play the synth, with velocity (with Rec on they are recorded as notes);
  • the pads play the drum rows or run Play, Play Solo Drums, Play solo Synth, Rec, Tap tempo, the metronome and the pattern change;
  • the knobs (and the MiniLab 3 faders) move the volumes, BPM, swing, humanize or any synth parameter (cutoff, resonance, envelopes, effects…).

To change an assignment press Learn on a pad or knob, then hit the pad or turn the knob on the keyboard. Each keyboard keeps its own assignments in the browser (you can use two at once); Factory assignments brings back the starting layout of the keyboard shown.

Import Drum Machine — macOS app

In the Machine panel of the macOS app you'll find Import Drum Machine. Choose a folder or a .zip archive of audio samples (WAV, AIFF, MP3, OGG, FLAC or M4A), name the new machine and confirm.

  • Samples are assigned to the pads in alphabetical order, up to 42 instruments. Each instrument's initial name comes from the file name.
  • With Edit machine you can change the machine's name and rename each instrument. Custom kits stay saved in the app.
  • Imported machines appear in the Imported machines section of the menu. With one selected you can use Delete machine; the app asks for confirmation before removing the files.
  • A custom kit can be chosen like any other machine; open patterns switch to the first available sample when a slot doesn't exist in the new kit.
  • This feature imports samples into the macOS app: the website keeps using the kits published on the server.

Per-step parameters

  • Probability: an active step plays 100, 75, 50 or 25% of the time ("Probability" mode above the grid).
  • Ratchets: 1, 2, 3 or 4 rapid hits inside the step; the ones after the first at 82%.
  • Flam: a grace note at 50% velocity, 24 ms before the main hit.
  • Tuning (+2, +5, +7, +12, −12, −7, −5 semitones), Decay (70, 40, 15) and Filter (70, 50, 30): they apply to that hit only and add to or replace the row settings. In Hits mode a dot in the top-right corner marks steps that have them.
  • They apply on playback, in the WAV export and in the stems; MIDI carries probability, ratchets and flams (tuning, decay and filter are sound, not notes).
  • Per-row length ("Len." menu): with fewer steps than the bar, the row loops on its own and doesn't realign at the end of the pattern (in the Song it restarts on every block). Steps beyond the length stay off.

Tempo and feel

  • MPC-style Swing, from 50% (straight) to 75%: 66% is the triplet feel. It lengthens the even sixteenths and shortens the odd ones.
  • Humanize: small random variations on every hit, up to ±10 ms and ±15% velocity; it also applies to WAV, stems and MIDI.
  • Per-row Feel, from −30 to +30 ms: pushes hits ahead or lays them back (snare “laid back”, kick “pushed”). When you load a generated pattern, the style's suggested feel is applied automatically.
  • Lengthening a pattern (8→16→32) repeats the rhythm in the new bars; shortening it cuts off the end.

Per row

Mute, solo, choke group (hi-hats cut each other off), tuning ±12 semitones, volume; in the ⚙ panel: decay, 4-pole low-pass filter with resonance, sample start, reverse, feel.

Rows are shared by all the patterns in the project: a row that's empty in the pattern you see can have hits in another, and choosing or generating a pattern adds rows for the sounds it needs. Remove empty rows (next to + Row) only removes rows with no hits in any pattern; cmd+Z undoes it.

Practical recipe: from four hits to a groove

  1. Put the kick on the four beats: 1, 5, 9 and 13.
  2. Put the snare on beats 2 and 4: 5 and 13.
  3. Add regular hi-hats, then remove one or two hits to create space.
  4. Turn one hi-hat into a ghost note or add an accent: change the feel without changing the shape.

Synthesizer

The Synthesizer adds a melodic and harmonic line locked to the drums. Draw notes in the piano roll, play them from the computer keyboard or a MIDI controller, record them during playback, transform them with generators and export the line as MIDI, WAV or MP3 with the song.

Start here

  1. Open the Synthesizer tab in the view bar. The Engine section contains the sound; the Pattern section contains the line you are editing.
  2. Choose a preset or start from a blank sound. KORG, Custom and Tone.js presets have separate banks; you can save and rename your own sounds.
  3. In Pattern use + New for an empty line, Duplicate for a variation, Clear to remove notes, Rename to name it and Steps to choose 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 or 256 steps.
  4. Choose Key and Scale for a musical reference. The scale filters the view when you enable Scale notes only; it does not move notes already written.
  5. Press Play to hear the synth with the drums. On enables or mutes the line; Solo hears the synth without drums; Panic immediately releases notes that are still held.

How parameters work together

  • For bass: Sine or Saw, lower VCO 2, Low-pass with low Cutoff, short Attack, high Sustain and Mono mode. Add a little Glide for legato.
  • For a pluck: Saw or Square, zero Attack, short Decay, zero Sustain, short Gate, positive Filter EG and a little Delay/Reverb.
  • For a pad: two oscillators, Poly or Unison, long Attack and Release, medium Cutoff, moderate Chorus and Reverb, Gate near 100%.
  • For an acid line: Saw, Mono, high Cutoff and Resonance, positive Filter EG, high Accent, medium Gate and Slide on connecting notes.
  • If the sound distorts, lower Drive, Volume, VCO/Multi levels or Synth volume in the Mixer. Effects add a tail but do not replace correct gain staging.

Song and sequencer

  • Each song block is a pattern repeated 1 to 64 times (− / + on the block). Drag blocks to reorder them; Duplicate, ← → and Delete act on the selected block.
  • Double-click a block: opens that pattern in the Grid for editing.
  • Click the timeline: the song starts (or jumps, if already playing) from the beginning of the clicked repeat.
  • The Pattern / Song switch next to Play decides what plays and what Multitrack MIDI, the SP-1200 kit MIDI and DAW packages export.
  • ● Record vocal: sing or play over the song, from the start or from the bar chosen on the timeline, with a count-in of 1 or 2 bars. The take is lined up with the beat (the Latency slider moves it earlier or later; double-click: automatic), you listen to it with the song and save it in the Sampler as a sample: the voice alone (mono, from the first beat) or the voice over the song. Use headphones.

Building a song in layers

  • Intro: use fewer rows or a shorter pattern to leave space.
  • Verse: keep the main groove and change one detail, such as the hi-hat.
  • Chorus: add density, crash, accents or a more open variation.
  • Break and outro: remove elements before the return or the ending.

Pattern engine

  • Each style is a "profile": BPM range, swing, weighted variants, allowed fills, required hits (checked by the tests) and a generator.
  • All randomness goes through a seeded generator: the same code always regenerates the same pattern.
  • Verse and chorus sections, 16 or 32 steps long, optional ending fill.
  • Suggestions: as soon as you choose a style (or "random" from a group or from all), section, length or fill, the library shows 8 patterns that differ from one another in place of the previous ones, ranked by interest: syncopation, accents and ghost notes, number of voices, variety within the bar, balanced density. The ● dots show the level. ↻ More suggestions generates another 8 with the same settings; favorites stay.
  • Similar: four close patterns (crossed with a pattern of the same style, plus small mutations).
  • Lock: keep some voices (for example kick and snare) and regenerate the others.
  • Arrange: derives intro, chorus, break and outro from a pattern.
  • Vary: in the library on a generated pattern, and in the Pattern panel on the one you are editing (even one written by hand).

Why generation is repeatable

The engine uses a seed inside the pattern code. Reopening the same code with the same style, length and variations gives the same pattern; changing one transformation gives you a traceable variant.

Variations

Styles

Export for Logic

MIDI (pattern or song)

  • Type 1 MIDI file, 96 PPQ (24 ticks per sixteenth), channel 10, project tempo included.
  • Notes: General MIDI map (36 kick, 38 snare, 42 closed hat, 46 open hat…), understood by any DAW. MIDI for Logic SP-1200 kit writes 36 + slot number instead (Type 0 file), i.e. the map of Logic's "SP-1200 Kit".
  • Velocity: accent 100%, normal 78%, ghost 40%, multiplied by the row volume.
  • Swing, feel and ratchets become real positions and notes; probability is decided on every pass, so each repeat of the song is a little different.

DAW packages (Logic, Ableton Live, REAPER, other DAWs)

  • A single zip file with: MIDI on the General MIDI map (36 kick, 38 snare, 42 closed hat, 46 open hat…), which Drummer, Drum Kit Designer and Drum Machine Designer understand without remapping.
  • One WAV stem per instrument (24 bit, 44.1 kHz, with row levels and effects), all the same length and starting from zero: dragged in together at bar 1 they stay aligned.
  • The machine's samples, with the GM note number before the name, to rebuild the kit in Drum Machine Designer.
  • A Leggimi.txt (read-me) with tempo, swing and the steps to import it into the chosen DAW. Swing and feel are already in the notes: leave quantize off.
  • The content is the same for every DAW; only the instructions change. REAPER also gets a ready-made .rpp project: tempo, one stem per track and a General MIDI track.
  • Other DAWs: instructions for FL Studio, Cubase, Studio One, Bitwig and GarageBand.
  • MIDI pattern and MIDI song download only the MIDI file on the GM map, without audio. MIDI for Logic SP-1200 kit uses the notes of the SP-1200 kit built in Logic (36 + slot) instead, and is meant for that kit only.

WAV

  • 44.1 kHz stereo, rendered offline with the same sounds, filters, levels and selected machine.
  • "WAV pattern" = 4 loops of the pattern; "WAV song" = the whole song; plus a 2-second tail.

MP3

  • Stereo export at 192 kbps, with the same sounds, filters, levels and machine as the WAV.
  • "MP3 pattern" = 4 loops of the pattern; "MP3 song" = the whole song; plus a 2-second tail.
  • Works in every browser, offline too, and in the app: the encoding is done by lamejs, a JavaScript port of LAME (LGPL license).

After an import

  1. Check the tempo: an audio file may have only an approximate detected BPM.
  2. Check the map: if kick or snare landed on the wrong row, fix the row before saving.
  3. Listen from the beginning and check accents; import converts velocity into normal, accent and ghost levels.
  4. Save a copy of the imported project before adding it to an existing project.

Projects, codes and links

  • Projects are saved with Save and reopened from the Saved projects tab (Recent projects without an account) by clicking the row or Open. From there you can also rename them and listen with the ▶ button, which loops the song (or the first pattern) without opening it.
  • With an account, saved projects are kept on the server and are the same in every browser and device you sign in from. They are saved in the browser first, so Save works offline too, and sync when the connection is back; the line under Saved projects shows the state. If the same project was changed on another device in the meantime, your version is kept as a copy next to the other one, so nothing is lost. Projects saved in a browser before this arrive in the account the first time you sign in there.
  • Undo and redo: cmd+Z / cmd+shift+Z, up to 80 steps.
  • Library favorites store only the pattern code: a few bytes that always regenerate the same pattern.
  • Code format: stile-16S-7F3A9B (style, steps, S verse / R chorus, F fill, seed), followed by the transformation steps: ~4C21 similar, ~R000005BEEF lock and regenerate, ~VP00A1 variation.
  • The Link button copies an address that reopens that pattern.
  • The site is a PWA: it installs as an app and also works offline.

What is saved

  • A project contains patterns, Song blocks, rows, levels, parameters, selected machine and groove settings.
  • A favorite saves the pattern code, not audio files: reopening it regenerates the pattern and uses the machine available at that time.
  • MIDI assignments and the theme are browser or controller settings, not part of the pattern code.

Troubleshooting

When something does not work, check the signal path first: source, connection, destination and transport. Most MIDI and audio problems are in one of these four places.

ProblemCheck firstFix
No soundMaster volume, browser not muted, machine available.Press Play, try a known pattern and check that the browser has an active audio output.
MIDI keyboard not visibleCompatible browser and MIDI permission.Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox, connect the controller before pressing Connect and close other apps using it.
DAW receives no notesPatternMachine port, track monitoring and MIDI channel.Turn on MIDI output, choose General MIDI or the correct map and check that the track is armed or monitored.
App does not follow tempoMIDI Clock and Start/Stop sent to the same output.Send clock from one DAW, select Follow DAW clock and restart transport from the DAW.
Imported audio is out of timeDetected BPM, file start and leading silence.Set the correct BPM, check the first hit and verify the grid before saving.

When to report a problem

If the problem remains, include the browser or DAW name, format used, MIDI path and steps to reproduce it. Avoid sending audio files or personal data unless they are necessary.

Known limitations

  • Sixteenth-note grid: no true triplets, so no jazz shuffle or 6/8. Swing only shifts the odd sixteenths.
  • BPM from 40 to 240: even the fastest blast beat stays below this ceiling.
  • Variations only act on rows with a recognized drum sound (the generator slots).
