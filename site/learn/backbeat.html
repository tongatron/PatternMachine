The backbeat

Clap along to almost any pop, rock, hip hop or R&B song and your hands land in the same two places: beats 2 and 4. That hit is the backbeat, usually a snare or a clap, and it's what turns a pulse into a groove. In this lesson you'll hear it, put it on the grid, play it live with your keyboard and then build it in PATTERN-MACHINE.

1 · Listen ✓ done

Kick and hi-hat stay the same; only the backbeat changes. Press Play (or the space bar) and switch while it plays.

Without a backbeat nothing answers the kick and the bar floats. Put a snare on 2 and 4 and the bar takes shape: the kick pushes on the strong beats, the snare answers in between. A finger snap does the same job with a lighter, brighter sound; soul and pop records often double the snare with claps, snaps or a tambourine.

Try it Count out loud “one, two, three, four” and clap only on two and four. That's the backbeat.

2 · Where it goes on the grid

A 16-step pattern is one bar of 4/4. The bar has four beats, and PATTERN-MACHINE splits each beat into four sixteenth notes, so a new beat starts every four steps: on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13, the ones with a number in the ruler above the grid. The steps in between are counted “e”, “and”, “a”: one e and a, two e and a…

Small number: the step. Big: how you count it. The lit squares are beats 2 and 4.

413backbeat

Beats 2 and 4 are steps 5 and 13: that's where the backbeat goes.

3 · Copy it ✓ done

Kick and hi-hat are already programmed and locked. Click the two marked squares on the Snare row, then press Play.

Good to know You can change steps while it plays: the change is heard on the next pass, just like in PATTERN-MACHINE. Clicking a lit step turns it off.

4 · Play it with your keyboard ✓ done

1kick2snare3hat4 QWER ASDF ZXCV

In PATTERN-MACHINE every row has a key. Rows 1 to 4 are 1 2 3 4, then come Q W E R, A S D F and Z X C V: sixteen keys laid out like the 4×4 pads of a sampler. The key follows the row's position, not its sound. Here the snare is the second row, so its key is 2.

  1. Press Play (space bar) and listen to the kick and hi-hat for a bar or two.
  2. Tap 2 on beats 2 and 4 to get the feel. Nothing is written yet.
  3. Turn on Rec: now every hit lands on the nearest step. When the snare sits on steps 5 and 13, you're done.

Each recorded hit shows how early or late it was. If one lands on the wrong step, press ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z on Windows) or click the step to remove it. Slow the tempo down if it helps: the backbeat stays on 5 and 13 at any speed.

Headphones Bluetooth headphones add a delay between what you play and what you hear: to record, use the speakers or wired headphones. No keyboard? Tap the 2 pad on the screen.

5 · Now in PATTERN-MACHINE ✓ done

Time for the real thing. The button opens the drum machine with this lesson's beat: kick and hi-hat programmed, snare row empty. A mission card in the corner tells you what to do and ticks each step as you go.

  1. Press Play.
  2. Turn on Rec and press 2 on beats 2 and 4, or click steps 5 and 13 on the Snare row.

What you learned

  • The backbeat is the hit on beats 2 and 4, usually a snare or a clap.
  • On a 16-step grid the beats are steps 1, 5, 9 and 13, so the backbeat goes on 5 and 13.
  • In PATTERN-MACHINE each row has a key (1 2 3 4 · Q W E R · A S D F · Z X C V). With Rec on, what you play is written on the nearest step.
  • You can edit while it plays, and ⌘Z undoes.

Going further

  • Half-time. A single snare on beat 3 (step 9) makes the same tempo feel twice as slow: it's the backbone of trap and dubstep, and it gets a lesson of its own.
  • Laid back. In the Drum Grid, open the snare row's ⋯ menu, then ⚙, and set Feel to about +10 ms: the backbeat sits just behind the beat, hip hop style. More in Grid and steps in the Guide.
  • Accent. alt+click turns a step into an accent (red): try accenting only beat 4.
